>>> STEP 1 -- confounding. Measured naively the period effect is OR = 0.961 (p = 0.095), i.e. apparently nothing. Once lifecycle depth and mix are controlled it becomes OR = 0.765 (p = 1.23e-33). The damage was hidden by composition drift, exactly as the cohort view showed.

>>> STEP 2 -- mediation. Adding support contacts and returns moves the period penalty from OR = 0.765 to OR = 0.793.
    13.2% of the controlled period effect (log-odds scale) is absorbed by service variables alone. Service quality is the mechanism, not an unexplained secular drift.
    Adding coupon dependency takes it to OR = 0.873 (p = 4.00e-09).
    Service + coupon dependency together account for 49.2% of the controlled period effect. The remaining 50.8% is what the order log simply cannot see -- delivery speed, stockouts, assortment changes. That residual is the case for instrumenting those systems.

Cross-check: the logit and the GBM agree on the top levers (lifecycle depth, category, coupon dependency, support contacts). Two model families with different assumptions landing in the same place is the strongest evidence available here short of an experiment.

IMPORTANT NUANCE -- cs_tickets carries only 6.8% of permutation importance, far below its odds ratio. That is not a contradiction: only 18.3% of orders have any ticket at all, so the variable is near-constant for most rows and shuffling it changes little globally. The ticket lever is DEEP but NARROW -- large effect on a small population. Phase 4 must size it on the affected population, not the whole base, or it will be badly overstated.

By contrast cum_discount_rate carries 15.4% of importance and applies to every order -- a shallower effect on a much wider population. That is the bigger pot of money.
