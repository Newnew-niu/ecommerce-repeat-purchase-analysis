==============================================================================
INITIATIVE 1 -- Tiered service recovery before the cliff
==============================================================================

Addressable in 2025Q1 extract: 323 orders at exactly 2 tickets, 104 at 3+. Grossed up: 12,920 and 4,160 cases/yr.
  AOV of 2-ticket orders  $128.44
  AOV of 3+-ticket orders $145.43
  (both well above the $87.66 blended AOV -- complaints concentrate in expensive baskets, which is what makes this worth doing)

Model counterfactual: 2 tickets -> 1 is worth +3.7 pp, 3 tickets -> 1 is worth +16.8 pp.
The raw observational 2->3 gap is ~18 pp. Not used: it embeds selection (customers already leaving complain more on the way out).

COST DESIGN -- this is where the initiative lives or dies. A human callback is fully loaded at ~$9-14. Tier A's modelled uplift cannot cover that, so Tier A must be automated. Tier B's 16.8 pp can. The tiering is not a presentation choice, it falls out of the break-even arithmetic.

Tier A: automated apology + priority re-routing + service credit, $0.80/case, $25k build. Assumes 0.45 downstream contacts avoided per case.
Tier B: senior-agent callback for escalated cases, $9.00/case, $20k setup. Assumes 1.10 downstream contacts avoided per case.

Electronics + Home: 27.9% of orders, $176.44 AOV, and the two worst repeat rates.
Model counterfactual -- zeroing support contacts in these categories is worth 2.62 pp of repeat (order-weighted). That is the CEILING for a fix that only addresses contacts.
Phase 2 root cause: Electronics avg tickets 0.24 -> 0.69 and Home 0.25 -> 0.42 between 2024-01 and 2025-06. Fulfilment and product-information problem, not a contact-centre capacity problem.

Cost-to-serve today: 0.510 tickets/order x $6.00 = $3.06 per order. A 30% reduction is assumed, i.e. 0.153 contacts avoided per order.

Unit cost $0.25/order (proactive delivery comms + spec-content remediation, amortised), $150k fixed (content rebuild, inbound QC sampling programme). Plus $0.35/order assumed reverse-logistics saving from better expectations.

==============================================================================
INITIATIVE 3 -- Wean the coupon-dependent segment
==============================================================================

Among users with >=3 prior orders:
  coupon-independent (<=40% discounted): 72.9% repeat, n = 9,439
  coupon-dependent   (>=60% discounted): 63.8% repeat, n = 2,135
  observational gap: 9.1 pp
  addressable: 669 orders in 2025Q1 extract -> 26,760/yr

Planning number: Phase 3 AME is 14.9 pp per full unit of dependency. A realistic programme shifts a user's coupon rate by ~0.25, giving 3.7 pp -- deliberately below the 9.1 pp observational gap.

REVERSE CAUSALITY WARNING: low-intent users seek coupons, so an unknown part of the observational gap is selection running backwards. Of the three initiatives this has the weakest causal identification. It is therefore specified as a randomised holdout test, not a rollout.

Unit economics: avg coupon ~12% of AOV = $10.52. Cutting dependency 0.25 with half sticking recovers $1.31/order in margin, against $0.90/order CRM cost.

--- What 'recovery' means in user terms ---
M+1 retention fell 7.6 pp (34.7% -> 27.2%). Against the 57,860 users acquired since 2024-11, that is ~4,374 users who would have returned under the old service level and did not.
The recommended portfolio delivers 2,424 incremental repeat orders/yr. This is a partial recovery by design: Phase 3 showed ~51% of the controlled decline sits in systems the order log cannot observe (delivery speed, stockouts, assortment). Closing the rest needs instrumentation we do not currently have.
